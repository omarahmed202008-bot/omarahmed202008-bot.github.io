CO₂ Eater — Hybrid Microalgae–Mineral Capture

A two-stage bioreactor combining mineral carbonation, microalgae biofixation, bottom-first pneumatic flow, and model-predictive adjustment of aeration and lighting.

Research problem and engineering inquiry

Industrial capture technologies can incur high energy requirements; conventional microalgal photobioreactors also face mass-transfer limitations, self-shading, low capture rates per volume, and a large footprint. The project's engineering inquiry asks how a combined chemical-biological reactor could improve CO₂ capture while managing electricity demand and remaining low cost.

It compares two background approaches: amine-solution chemical absorption (mature technology but solvent-regeneration energy costs) and conventional open/tubular microalgae systems (biological fixation with photosynthetic and land-use constraints).

Two-stage bottom-first design

  1. Lower mineral bed: incoming CO₂-containing air is introduced through a porous diffuser into a calcium-hydroxide [Ca(OH)₂] chamber for mineral carbonation / chemical pretreatment. A representative reaction is Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O.
  2. Vertical upflow transfer: partly treated air travels through a central channel into the upper section, allowing bubble movement to assist mixing without a separate mechanical stirrer.
  3. Upper biological section: microalgae take up CO₂ through photosynthesis with light and environmental control.

The mineral bed and the microalgae chamber are complementary; the poster's illustrated flow direction is lower-to-upper.

Electronics and sensor architecture

  • ESP32-WROOM-32: dual-core controller, described as allocating telemetry/Wi-Fi and time-critical control to separate processing tasks.
  • MH-Z19B: nondispersive infrared CO₂ monitoring via UART, source-described 0–5000 ppm range.
  • DS18B20: waterproof temperature sensing over a one-wire interface.
  • BH1750: light-intensity sensing over I²C; the poster relates it to illumination control.
  • IRF540N MOSFET and 12-V DC air pump: pulse-width-modulated gas-flow adjustment.
  • LED arrays: dimming in response to ambient light and predicted microalgal demand.

The poster contrasts the MH-Z19B NDIR sensor with an MQ-135 metal-oxide gas sensor in terms of drift, selectivity, response time and stability. Manufacturer specifications and its sensor-comparison claims require separate validation.

AI controller and mathematics

The system description applies a one-dimensional Kalman filter for sensor smoothing and a receding-horizon model predictive controller. The controller reportedly predicts uptake demand over a 10-minute window, modulates pump duty cycle and LED output, and recalculates the capture-efficiency metric every 30 seconds.

  • Capture efficiency: η = (C_in − C_out) / C_in.
  • Energy efficiency ratio: EER = mass of CO₂ captured (g) / energy consumed (kWh).
  • Classification accuracy: (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN); AI training/testing classification tables are presented separately in the poster.

A clear distinction is needed between measuring CO₂ concentration reduction and demonstrating mass of permanently sequestered carbon.

Experimental protocol from the poster

The poster describes three 48-hour continuous operating cycles, comparing a fixed mode with a constant 100% power policy against an adaptive AI-modulated mode. It states a controlled inlet concentration around 480 ± 10 ppm, along with comparisons of energy consumption, concentration-based capture efficiency, and biomass absorbance. The report does not include the underlying time-series CSV files.

Standards, limitations and future development

The research poster discusses ISO 14001 in the context of environmental management and ISO 16075 when describing potential reuse of treated water/biomass. These are standards references, not evidence of certification, environmental compliance testing, or safe biomass reuse.

Constraints requiring further validation include sensor calibration, reliable mass-balance accounting, chemical-reactant exhaustion, long-term algal culture stability, full power accounting, and genuine carbon storage duration. The proposed system is stationary and modular rather than a proven industrial-scale installation.
