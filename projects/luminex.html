Luminex — Portable E-Waste Spectrophotometer

A low-cost visible-light absorbance measurement system assembled from recovered optoelectronics and standalone embedded circuitry, with calibration against known dye concentrations.

Scientific question and motivation

Laboratory spectrophotometers can be costly, while discarded electronics contain optical and sensing components that could be reused for educational instruments. Luminex addresses both issues by repurposing electronic waste into a portable, stand-alone absorbance reader suitable for demonstration of Beer–Lambert behavior.

It specifically focuses on visible-light colorimetry; although the source sometimes calls the apparatus a spectrophotometer, it does not demonstrate a continuously scanned spectral range.

Optical design and light isolation

The optical path is RGB LED light source → focusing/collimating optics → sample cuvette → photodiode detector. The LED color can be selected to suit the sample's absorbance. A lens salvaged from a DVD optical unit is described as helping produce a more controlled beam.

An early transparent housing admitted ambient light and produced fluctuations reportedly around ±15%. The team redesigned the enclosure using a matte-black internal optical tunnel within a transparent exterior. The poster describes an interlocking lid and a narrow 2-mm slit as measures for reducing stray light.

Electronics and signal processing

  • Recovered photodiode from an optical mouse, with a BPW34 device also named elsewhere in the source.
  • LM358 operational amplifier used as a transimpedance amplifier (TIA), converting small detector current into a measurable voltage.
  • ATmega328P standalone microcontroller with a 10-bit ADC (approximately 4.9 mV/count on a 5 V reference).
  • RGB LED, LCD readout, regulated 5 V supply, and electronic-noise suppression components.

The poster mentions both 100 kΩ and 10 MΩ TIA feedback resistors in different sections, and inconsistent photodiode descriptions. The exact installed values should be checked against the final schematic before formal publication.

Measurement equations

  • Transmittance: T = I / I₀, where I₀ is the blank reference and I is light transmitted through sample.
  • Absorbance: A = −log₁₀(I / I₀).
  • Beer–Lambert relation: A = εlc in the linear range for a fixed path length and wavelength.
  • Amplifier approximation: voltage magnitude proportional to photocurrent multiplied by feedback resistance, with polarity depending on the op-amp arrangement.
  • Electrical power: P = VI.

The poster describes firmware averaging 50 ADC samples before computing the intensity ratio and displaying absorbance.

Experimental protocol

  1. Warm up and stabilize the selected visible LED channel.
  2. Use a distilled-water blank to measure the incident reference level I₀.
  3. Test five standard red-dye concentrations: 6.25%, 12.5%, 25%, 50%, and 100% (relative concentration labels from the source).
  4. Repeat each concentration measurement five times.
  5. Calculate absorbance, plot it against concentration and fit a regression line.
  6. Compare measurement accuracy and repeatability with the stated ≤5% design requirement.

Design iterations and lessons

The initial optical design exposed the detector to room illumination. The nested opaque tunnel, internal shielding, alignment improvements and noise-filtering additions were introduced to reduce this error. The source also describes added decoupling capacitors near the microcontroller. This sequence illustrates why optical geometry and reference stability matter as much as the absorbance equation.

Limitations and proposed improvements

Constraints include the use of broad visible RGB channels instead of a calibrated spectrally narrow scan, dependence on optical alignment and blank-reference stability, uncertain exact TIA component values, and a small calibration set. The poster proposes Wi-Fi/ESP-based cloud logging, improved spectral resolution, and UV/IR emitters for future multi-spectral analysis. Those additions are future plans, not demonstrated capabilities.
