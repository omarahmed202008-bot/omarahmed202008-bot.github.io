Agrifusion 2.0 — Predictive Plant-Stress Monitoring

A multimodal plant-health platform combining soil sensing, environmental monitoring, VOC-responsive signals, leaf imagery, ML classification, and forecasting to support preemptive irrigation.

Problem, motivation and engineering question

Conventional agriculture often identifies stress after leaves visibly wilt or develop disease symptoms. Soil-only thresholds and occasional manual inspection may miss earlier physiological changes, leading to mistimed watering and inefficient use of resources. The proposal asks whether a low-cost, autonomous, edge-capable system can combine visual, environmental, and VOC-related signals to forecast plant stress before visible symptoms while controlling irrigation demand.

Biological indicators and sensing

  • Root-zone water: capacitive soil-moisture sensing converted toward volumetric water content after calibration.
  • Climate: temperature and humidity sampling to interpret environmental stress.
  • Gas-response signals: MQ-135-type metal-oxide sensor array used as a broad gas-response proxy rather than a chemically selective VOC analyzer.
  • Leaf imaging: ESP32-CAM captures leaf color/texture information, with green-index features and image embeddings.
  • Optional electrophysiology: discussed as an additional exploratory modality, not demonstrated as essential in the available result tables.

The poster discusses baseline resistance ratios (R_s/R_0) and sensor drift correction. Claims that a general MQ-135 response can identify specific plant-emitted compounds require specialist validation.

Hardware and operation

The proposed hardware combines an ESP32-S3 processing board, ADS1115-style analog sampling, camera module, environmental sensors, optional solar-assisted mobile four-wheel rover, peristaltic pumps and MOSFET drivers. The design aims to scan multiple plants rather than install a complete sensing node on every pot.

An example closed-loop decision sequence is: acquire measurements → preprocess and fuse signals → infer plant condition and forecast risk → trigger targeted watering if the risk threshold is exceeded → record the action locally and report it to a dashboard. The poster describes a 30-second sensing/inference cycle and a predicted-risk threshold above 0.7.

Learning architecture and research aims

The poster discusses Random Forest, LightGBM and MobileNet-based image embeddings for classifications of Healthy, Needs Water, and Possible Disease. Fused tabular features and image embeddings feed models that distinguish current plant condition. An LSTM or temporal convolutional architecture is described for 6–12-hour forecasts. TinyML quantization is part of the edge-deployment plan.

The explicit design objectives include a >0.90 F1 target, pre-symptomatic stress forecasting, closed-loop irrigation and >20% water saving versus less adaptive strategies. Targets and experimental findings are different things.

Poster-reported dataset and validation

The poster describes 11,000 synchronized historical data points associated with an earlier stationary Agrifusion prototype, an 80/20 validation split, and software-in-the-loop testing. It presents results as retrospective model evaluation against historical sensor and moisture behavior, not as an independently established large-scale farm outcome.

The poster concludes that six-hour lead time was successfully validated in its sequence analysis. Its software-in-the-loop irrigation example lists 28 daily timer-based events, 18 reactive events and 12 optimized events. Estimated relative water usage is shown as 100% baseline, 78% reactive, and approximately 65% optimized. These are modeled/projected quantities.

A later slide presentation uses an approximately 94.2% F1 figure and a projected 40% water reduction, which do not match the poster's 96.6% overall F1 and approximate 35% modeled saving exactly. The portfolio retains each document's respective numbers and avoids presenting either as independently confirmed field performance.

Evaluation metrics, deployment and safety

Classification evaluation uses precision, recall and F1 because class imbalance can make accuracy misleading; forecasting uses absolute and root-mean-square error concepts. The poster references IEC 60529 enclosure-rating considerations and safety discussion for agricultural automation. It does not provide an independent IP65 certificate or field-safety assessment.

Important limitations include environmental confounding of gas sensors, lighting variation in images, potential data leakage in time-series train/test splits, generalization from stationary data to a moving rover, inference memory limits, and the difference between a simulated controller and a physically measured water-saving outcome.
